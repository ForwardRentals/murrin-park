Field Notes · Murrin Park

A Murrin Park Day Trip From Vancouver

Murrin is small, so the trick is stacking it with the right extras. Here's a simple plan: when to arrive, what to do in the park, and what to pair it with nearby.

Murrin Provincial Park is a roadside granite bowl about 10 km south of Squamish: Browning Lake, climbing walls and a one-hour ridge loop. On its own it's a stop, not a destination. Stack it right and it's a great day.

Getting there and parking

Murrin is right on Highway 99, roughly an hour from Vancouver. The lot is small. On hot summer Saturdays it can fill before 10 a.m., and the gate may close when it's full. Your options:

  • Arrive before 9 a.m.
  • Come on a weekday.
  • Have a backup. If it's full, carry on to Squamish and come back later.

The park is day use only: no camping, no fires.

The plan

Morning: the Jurassic Ridge loop

Start with the Jurassic Ridge loop while it's cool: about 2 km, around an hour, with two viewpoints over Howe Sound.

Late morning: watch the climbers

By now there are usually ropes up on the walls. Watch from a respectful distance; here's how to do it without getting in the way.

Midday: swim and paddle Browning Lake

Browning Lake is small, calm and motor-free, which makes it one of the easiest first paddleboard or canoe outings in the Sea to Sky. There are well-known jump spots on the far shore; check the depth before you go off. Squamish Canoe Rental delivers canoes and boards to the lot, or you can pick up at their Squamish base on the drive.

Add-ons nearby

  • Porteau Cove: a few minutes south, with a beach, a pier and big Howe Sound views. A great sunset spot on the drive home.
  • A boat on Howe Sound: Squamish Water Taxi runs Howe Sound tours from Squamish, including sunset departures from Porteau Cove.
  • Squamish: lunch, coffee and the Stawamus Chief, ten minutes north.
  • Shannon Falls: a short walk to a big waterfall just south of Squamish.
  • Stay over: Murrin has no camping, so pair it with Alice Lake or Porteau Cove if you want a night out (book ahead).

What to pack

  • Swimsuit, towel and water shoes.
  • Trail shoes for the loop.
  • Water, lunch and sunscreen.
  • A bag to pack out your garbage.

Honest expectations

Murrin really is small. The lake takes about ten minutes to paddle end to end. That's the appeal: you can do the loop, a swim and a paddle and still be home for dinner.

Questions

How far is Murrin Park from Vancouver?

Murrin Provincial Park is on Highway 99 about 10 km south of Squamish, roughly an hour's drive from Vancouver depending on traffic.

Can you camp at Murrin Park?

No. Murrin is day use only, with no camping or fires. Many people pair it with Alice Lake or Porteau Cove if they want to stay overnight.

When does the Murrin Park parking lot fill up?

On hot summer weekends it can fill before 10 a.m. Arrive before 9 or come on a weekday.
