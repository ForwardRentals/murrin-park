Climbing at Murrin Park

Murrin is a real climbing crag, not just a viewpoint. Here's how to watch respectfully, stay safe below the walls, and how to try climbing yourself.

At Murrin, the swimmers and the climbers share one granite bowl. By mid-morning there are usually ropes on the walls above Browning Lake. Watching is half the fun of a visit, as long as you know where to stand.

A real crag

Murrin is one of the well-known climbing areas around Squamish, with routes spread across several walls, including the Petrifying Wall area. Climbers come for short, quality routes close to the road. To them, the base of a wall is a workplace: ropes, gear and a belayer whose attention needs to stay on their partner.

How to watch without getting in the way

  • Never walk or stand directly under a climber. Rocks, gear and dropped items fall. Helmets are worn for a reason.
  • Don't step over ropes or packs. Walk around.
  • Don't distract the belayer. Save the questions until the climber is down. Most climbers are happy to chat then.
  • Keep kids and dogs back. A curious dog at the base of a wall is a hazard to everyone.
  • Find a spot across the lake. Some of the best views of the walls are from the far shore or from the water.

Watching from the water

A canoe or paddleboard on Browning Lake gives you a front-row seat without being in anyone's way. Squamish Canoe Rental delivers to the Murrin lot.

Want to try climbing?

Climbing outdoors is safe when the system is set up and checked by someone who knows what they're doing. If you're new:

  • Hire a certified guide. They bring the gear, set the ropes, teach you the basics and keep you safe. Look for guides certified by the ACMG (Association of Canadian Mountain Guides).
  • Learn indoors first. A climbing gym is a great place to learn to tie in and belay.
  • Don't improvise. Climbing on "a rope we found" or scrambling up walls without protection is how people get badly hurt.

Leave no trace at the crag

  • Stay on established trails at the base of walls; the soil and plants are fragile.
  • Pack out everything, including tape and food scraps.
  • Keep noise reasonable. Climbers need to hear each other.

The park sits in Sḵwx̱wú7mesh (Squamish) Nation territory. Treat it with care.

Questions

Is Murrin Park good for climbing?

Yes. Murrin is a well-known Squamish-area crag with a range of routes on granite walls around Browning Lake, including the Petrifying Wall area.

Can beginners climb at Murrin Park?

Beginners should go with a certified guide or an experienced friend who can set up and check the system. Hiring a guide is the safest way to try it.

Is it safe to watch climbers at Murrin Park?

Yes, from a sensible distance. Never stand or walk directly under someone climbing, since rocks and gear can fall, and keep kids and dogs away from the base of the walls.
